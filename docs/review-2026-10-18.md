### 2.3 Inefficiencies

- Agent.step was measured on a machine at load 35–60 on 8 cores, so read these as an upper bound, probably 2–3× too high. Cost per drone per simulated second: 16 ms (demo), 34 ms (valley), **69 ms (Munich)**. The median step takes 0.1 ms, but **spikes reach 225–248 ms on Munich and the valley, more than the 100 ms tick**.
  - The spikes come from `belief.tracks` (about 8 ms per call on the demo grid, 70 ms on Munich's 250×350 grid; 5.5 of 12 s of agent time in a 120 s demo profile) and `merge_overlapping` (41k `_overlap` calls).
  - `add_evidence` touches the whole grid for every reading (about 12 ms each on Munich).
  - Fixes: recompute `tracks` only after evidence changes; limit each reading to its bounding box (hearing range or RF ring); decay lazily.
  - With those fixes the "limited compute" slide can be true: say "X ms per drone per sim-second" from an idle machine.
- State messages are 36 KB of msgpack (30 KB of it heatmaps) at 10 Hz, and `radio_proc` unpacks all of it for positions. Use a separate `pos.` topic.
- `earshot eval` has no `--jobs`, so "100 runs today" is serial (the 30-run sound eval took 33 min). Only `tune` uses a pool.

**Top five judge pokes:**
1. "You put microphones at an airport and assumed 3 a.m. silence."
2. "Only two drones hear it, 120 m apart, crossing at 30°: how is that triangulation?"
3. "Your RF sensor is a drone oracle."
4. "Is your radio legal in Munich?"
5. "Where is the wind?"

**Feasibility verdict.** The *core* is sound physics: cheap distributed acoustic sensing works in the field (Ukraine's Sky Fortress: about 9,500–14,000 phone mics at $400–1,000 each). Small arrays hear a quadcopter at about 100–300 m in quiet conditions. Perching saves 30–70 % power in research. What is not realistic today: the 40 dB airport, RF as a near-perfect drone detector, the 100 m camera, instant perching, and EU radio settings. Fixed, these turn into the best demo material: "the swarm knows when it's deaf."

**Realism changes that stay demo-able:**
1. Noise that changes over time: a 55 dB base, +25 dB bursts of about 30 s every 90–180 s, and a per-roof offset from the nearest APU stand.
2. An RF-silent intruder: a Shahed-class source (about 115 dB(A), no RF) and a fiber-optic or autonomous quad.
3. Honest RF: 915 MHz at 20 dBm with a −100 dBm floor; the threshold at +3 dB; a Wi-Fi nuisance source; RF-only readings at about 40 % drone.
4. An EU radio profile (125 kHz, 1 % duty cycle, 14 dBm), shown shedding Observations first. That turns a legal problem into a degraded-comms demo.
5. Perched bearing σ of 10°, mirror-bearing outliers off the nearest wall, wind noise of about 20·log10(v/2) + 50 dB, and a gust-dependent chance of a failed perch.
6. Camera at 50 m with `0.95·exp(−(d/50)²)`, intruders at 15–20 m/s, and a battery in Wh.

## 4. Product

**Market:** counter-UAS is estimated at $4.8–9.2B in 2026, about 74 % military. Price anchors:
- DroneShield fixed sites: hundreds of thousands to over $1M, plus about 10 % a year in SaaS.
- Riga Airport's system: about €5M.
- DFS estimate for Germany's major airports: about €500M.
- Sky Fortress: $400–500 per pole.

Incidents: Munich closed twice on Oct 2–4, 2025 (about 3,000 and 6,500 passengers); Copenhagen, Oslo and Aalborg in Sep 2025; 420 incursions over US bases. In none of them was the drone attributed. (The concept's "10,000 passengers" figure is the two Munich nights combined; say it that way.)

**Competitors:**

| Player | Sensor | Gap Earshot targets |
|---|---|---|
| Dedrone, DroneShield | RF | miss drones that don't transmit |
| Hidden Level | passive RF as a service | closest business model; RF only |
| Echodyne, Robin | radar | expensive; birds |
| Squarehead | fixed acoustic | fixed, short range |
| Sky Fortress | 9,500 fixed phone mics | dense everywhere, static |

No counter-drone network built on perching drones was found, so "to our knowledge" holds.

**Two concept claims to fix:**
- The "July 2026 rule exempts acoustic sensors from certification" could not be confirmed. The SAFER SKIES Act and the DOJ/DHS rule effective Jul 1, 2026 tie detection to certified *government* operators. Say instead: "passive, detection-only, operated by or for the certified agency."
- Flying your own drones near a runway is a regulatory liability. Expect "you fix drones over airports with more drones over airports?"

**Make it a product:**
- **Mobility as a mode.** The same sensor head and the same map and decision software, bolted to a roof where flying is restricted (airports) or flying and perching where allowed (prisons, events, bases).
- **Beachheads, ranked:**
  1. Prisons: named in SAFER SKIES, a perimeter of a few hundred metres that matches acoustic range, a recurring measurable pain, no conflict with air traffic.
  2. Stadiums and temporary events: mobility pays, deployment is short.
  3. Military bases. Airports come after these, as a data feed into airport police's existing system.
- **Business model:** detection as a service per km² per month. Label all numbers as assumptions.
- **Export standard tracks** (SAPIENT/ASTERIX-style) into existing command-and-control systems such as UNIFY.C2 or Lattice: sell the layer, not a console.
- **Evidence package per alert:** bearings, camera clip and map snapshot for police. Attribution is the unsolved problem in every 2025 incident.
- **Coverage planner:** given a site and a budget, where the nodes perch or mount, and the expected time to find a drone. It doubles as a sales tool.

## 6. Demo-risk checklist

From the dashboard and Webots review, ranked:

1. **The whole stack, dashboard included, exits when the scenario ends** (`world_proc.py:96`, `cli.py:255-261`). `muc.yaml` runs 480 s, `demo.yaml` 360 s, and a pitch plus Q&A runs past that. I saw it end my live run ("scenario finished after 240 s … stopped"). Launch with `--duration 3600`, or let the world idle at the end.
2. **If Webots crashes, the run ends** (`webots.py:210` raises `SystemExit`). The HUD keeps the last JPEG with a blinking REC dot, so it looks live but is frozen. Fall back to the backend's own `self.twin`, and clear the frames after 3 s with no new one.
3. **"Verify flights" counts every dispatch, including ones later outbid** (`world_proc.py:118`). Demo seed 0 shows 31, of which 19 were handed over. **"Confirmed" counts each drone's confirmation, not distinct intruders** (seed 0: 4 events at about 2 places). Count distinct tasks and intruders, and relabel to "Verifications".
4. **"Probability 100 %"** is shown for saturated log-odds (`hud.html:924`). Show ">99 %" and lead with the evidence in log-odds.
5. **The HUD loads its font from Google Fonts** (`hud.html:7-8`), which blocks first paint on venue WLAN and contradicts "works offline". Ship the woff2 file.
6. **A third live drone (`d` key) steals the first one's 3D node** (`webots.py:35`, `LIVE_SLOTS = 2`). The Verifier then dismisses a real drone on stage. Raise the slot count or refuse the add.
7. **Webots start-up race:** a fixed 3 s sleep (`cli.py:230`) before `Supervisor()`. Munich may take longer to load; add a bounded retry.
8. The project CLAUDE.md still says "base nodes only (ADR 0005)", but the world builder now uses EXTERNPROTOs from GitHub. **Cache them on both Macs**; the venue network may be slow or offline.
9. UX for the pitch:
   - Without cameras, make the map the hero (a `body.nocam` layout, or project `/classic`).
   - Put a one-line summary of the top Suspicion in Live view.
   - Make the decision ticker readable (it is 12 px and cut off).
   - Show a toast when a hotkey is pressed.
   - Label the heatmap colours by class.

## 9. 10:30 checkpoint draft

- **What we're building:** a swarm of cheap drones that land on roofs to listen, fuse sound bearings and passive RF into a shared probability map over a low-bandwidth mesh, and decide among themselves where to listen and who flies to verify. Today: active listening, meaning choosing the perch that maximises information under changing noise and position uncertainty.
- **Stack:** Python; a per-drone Bayes grid; a LoRa-like simulated mesh with ≤ 40 B HMAC frames; Webots on a 1:1 Munich Airport model (LoD2 + OSM); ReSpeaker arrays with SRP-PHAT; an EfficientAT sound classifier.
- **Hardest technical challenge:** choosing perch sites by expected information gain in a decentralised way, without double-counting relayed evidence and while radio links drop.
